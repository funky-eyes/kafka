# Recoverable window for independently confirmed WAL blocks

## Problem and scope

The experimental parallel-block WAL currently acknowledges a block's durable future only after `RingBlockCommitCoordinator` has forced a checkpoint for the complete preceding physical prefix. A slow block for partition A therefore holds the confirmation, follower Fetch and high-watermark progress of an already-forced block for partition B. `PartitionDurableOffsetTracker` already sequences Kafka offsets within each partition, but its input future is still gated by that global checkpoint.

AutoMQ 1.3.4 separates each Stream's completion sequence from its in-memory global trim position and recovers records by scanning a persisted window. Its `S3Storage.recoverContinuousRecords` reads recovered payloads, checks them against opening Stream end offsets and assembles each Stream's continuous records. See the pinned source analysis in [the comparison](automq-1.3.4-wal-comparison.md). Our implementation must preserve Kafka's own ISR and leader-epoch rules and recover from an independent follower's WAL when the leader and its disk are lost; the caller-supplied boundaries used by the internal classifier are not yet authoritative Kafka state.

This document specifies the next design boundary. It does not change the current format or claim that independent block confirmation is implemented.

The initial implementation adds an internal, distinct checkpoint layout with a forced scan ceiling and a bounded, read-only physical-window inspector. The inspector validates the stored checkpoint and strict prefix first, then reports checksum-valid candidates and unclassified ranges; neither is published to Kafka or treated as proof that a hole can be reclaimed. A visitor must discard staged results if a later read fails. Ordinary opening still fails closed whenever the ceiling exceeds the strictly checkpointed tail. To avoid silently falling back behind a possibly acknowledged window, the new layout requires at least one valid checkpoint copy from each bank; losing an entire bank fails closed. Broker WAL opening explicitly rejects this layout even when its window is empty, without repairing or modifying the file. Existing layouts retain their prior format and confirmation rules. Authoritative per-partition reconciliation and independent confirmation remain to be implemented.

An internal snapshot entry point stages only bounded metadata and returns it after the complete inspection succeeds. It preserves the WAL identity, checkpoint generation, unclassified ranges, block/group boundaries, partition, leader epoch, Kafka offsets and WAL locations without retaining block payloads. It rechecks the checkpoint generation after scanning; callers must still hold exclusive ownership and prevent concurrent writes or reuse, because that recheck cannot detect data changes inside an unchanged window. The snapshot is evidence for reconciliation, not authority to publish records or reclaim space. An internal read-only recovery lease now acquires the same cooperative exclusive WAL ownership used by the writer before opening and scanning the file, and retains it until the reader closes. It discovers the format and identity from checksummed headers, rejects older formats, and releases ownership on failed scans. This lease is not wired into Broker startup and has no ownership-transfer or reconciliation mechanism; callers must not use an escaped snapshot after it closes. The existing advisory lock does not fence non-cooperating writers or alternate hard-link paths. The lease can also classify candidate groups against caller-supplied partition start and required-end offsets and a single allowed leader epoch. It retains unclassified physical ranges, validates group IDs against the strict committed prefix, preserves complete groups, and quarantines gaps, epoch mismatches, unsupported cross-partition groups, control records and partitions without boundaries. An incomplete required end is reported separately. This classification is not proof that the supplied boundaries are authoritative, that a candidate was forced, or that any record may be published; even a partition with no reported issue cannot be published until those independent proofs and Broker startup integration exist. Multi-epoch and TRUNCATE recovery remain unsupported by this restricted pass.

The lease can now reread an inspected candidate and stage one classified partition's complete contiguous groups, with an explicit payload-byte limit. It checks the full stored checkpoint state before and after each block read, checks the saved block checksum and metadata against the snapshot, and validates the checkpoint once more before returning the complete result. A later failure returns no partial result. The snapshot and staging limits bound item count, record count, payload bytes and individual block size; record metadata and temporary block buffers add overhead to the payload limit. CRC detects accidental damage, not a malicious collision, and advisory ownership still cannot stop a non-cooperating writer. This supplies bytes for later reconciliation; it neither establishes the caller's boundary as authoritative nor enables windowed Broker recovery or early durable confirmation.

## Required invariants

* Kafka acceptance, physical block force, partition confirmation, replica confirmation, high watermark and reclaim head are separate positions. Early `acks=1` acceptance is explicitly allowed to precede every durable copy; it does not prove failover survival.
* A partition may confirm a forced group only after every earlier group of that partition has completed and its Kafka state is published. A slow or failed group in another partition must not prevent that confirmation.
* A follower may fetch only leader-confirmed data, and must not advertise its own durable progress until its independent WAL can recover the same records. Kafka's ISR and epoch rules continue to determine the high watermark.
* Every block called recoverably durable must lie within a recovery window whose upper bound was itself persisted before that block became eligible for confirmation. The window must be bounded by the physical ring and the persisted reclaim head.
* Recovery must reject stale physical generations, torn or corrupt data, malformed groups and invalid epoch/offset transitions. It must not silently discard a potentially committed record merely because another block is incomplete.
* A WAL range may be reused only after its data is covered by authoritative committed remote objects, the corresponding reclaim head is persisted, and readers holding the old range have exited.

## Proposed window protocol

Introduce a separately versioned checkpoint layout rather than changing the meaning of existing version-1 or version-2 files. Its durable state needs at least a WAL identity, metadata generation, reclaim head `H`, a strictly validated contiguous prefix ending at `T`, and an exclusive scan ceiling `C`. With current in-memory reservation position `R`, maintain `H <= T <= R <= C` and `C - H <= ringCapacity`. Any design that changes these quantities must prove the corresponding wrap and overflow rules.

Before reserving outside the current window, extend `C` in a batched checkpoint and force the new checkpoint before a block in the extension may complete its recoverable-durability future. Once `C` covers a reservation, the worker may write and force the block and deliver that block's result independently. Per-partition sequencing controls publication to Fetch and replication; a separate contiguous physical frontier controls reclaim. The durable contiguous prefix may still advance in batches, but a block outside it must not depend on that advancement for partition confirmation.

The ceiling is a bound, not evidence that every byte inside the window was written. Block framing must validate absolute logical address, WAL identity/incarnation, complete length, checksum and atomic-group contents. A speculative window can contain unwritten sectors, wrap padding, a torn block, and independently completed later blocks. Corruption inside the durable prefix remains a hard failure. Outside that prefix, recovery must distinguish provably unused or stale sectors from ambiguous damage; ambiguous data must be quarantined and reconciled with an independent replica, not silently treated as an ordinary hole.

Recovery is two-phase. First scan and validate physical candidates without publishing them as Kafka-visible data. Then reconcile each partition against an authoritative starting boundary, committed remote coverage, Kafka leader epoch, and replica state; publish only a contiguous safe sequence. A later valid block for partition B may survive a hole for A. A later block for A must not become visible across an A-offset gap. Where metadata or an independent replica cannot prove a required boundary, fail or fence the affected recovery rather than inventing an offset or silently losing committed data.

The present Broker lifecycle makes the ordering important: `CoordinatedRingWalAdapter.reopen` opens the checkpoint and starts its assembler before `SharedStorageEngine` replays the local WAL; `S3SharedStorageExtension.initializeRemotePlane` subsequently replays authoritative object metadata and calls `SharedUnifiedLogFactory.reconcileRemoteStateAfterMetadataReplay`. The factory currently rebuilds Kafka-visible log and producer/epoch state from the shared read view. A new format must therefore introduce a read-only, write-fenced recovery phase across those existing entry points, rather than inserting speculative records into `walIndex` in the engine constructor. Object coverage alone also cannot certify the leader epoch or independent follower durability; those proofs must come from the corresponding Kafka replication state before any candidate is published.

An internal restart fence advances the next reservation past the entire previously persisted ceiling and treats the unresolved interval as occupied. Reservations cannot cross the currently forced ceiling, and the window writer owns group ID allocation so that IDs from incomplete earlier writes cannot be reused. Its conservative first ID after restart is `lastCommittedGroup + (C - T) + 1`: each previously allocated group consumed at least one byte in the window. Wrap padding only increases this bound. This depends on exclusive file ownership and every window write, including writes before restart, using the fenced allocator; opening a second checkpoint object does not itself enforce that contract. A single checkpoint object rejects multiple allocators. Arithmetic exhaustion fails closed. The quarantined interval blocks contiguous commitment and reclamation; it is not automatically considered empty. The writer's completion proves only a local data force, not Kafka visibility, a committed checkpoint, or an independent replica. This isolated mechanism is not integrated into Broker startup or evidence of safe production recovery; authoritative reconciliation, an ownership-transfer protocol, and a way to resolve and reclaim quarantined intervals are still required. Until then a full ring applies backpressure. Shutdown and leadership cancellation under that backpressure also need Broker-level validation.

## Integration order

1. Define and test the versioned checkpoint, window extension, incarnation and restart rules independently of changing Kafka confirmation semantics. Old layouts must reopen with their existing strict-prefix behavior; migration must be explicit and crash-safe.
2. Implement physical-window scanning and per-partition reconciliation with an authoritative recovery boundary. Retain enough physical metadata to pin readers and prove reclaim across holes.
3. Introduce a distinct block-forced completion signal without weakening existing `SharedWal` durable futures. Only after restart proof is complete may the new format use it to advance partition confirmation.
4. Wire confirmed leader positions into follower Fetch and Kafka HW while preserving epoch fencing, and validate RF1, RF2, `acks=1`, `acks=all`, retries and lifecycle transitions. Keep unsupported producer-state and transaction cases on the existing safe path until separately proven.

## Required failure cases

* Pause block A before force, force later block B for a different partition, and prove B can be confirmed and replicated without waiting for A. Crash and recover the broker with B still present.
* Repeat with A and B in the same partition; B must not pass the missing earlier offset. Exercise leader change, ISR change and local log replacement while confirmations are queued.
* Crash before and after window checkpoint force, partial writes, data force, per-partition publication, remote-object commit and reclaim-head force. Repeat at wrap boundaries, including repeated crashes and reused physical sectors.
* Corrupt a previously confirmed block, including its header, and prove recovery either obtains a validated independent copy or fences the affected recovery. A corruption must not be reclassified as an unacknowledged hole merely to continue.
* With full ISR, kill a leader and permanently remove its local WAL before and after a follower has durably confirmed the record. Distinguish the permitted early-ACK loss window from the post-replication survival guarantee. Run NIO and AIO on distinct failure domains.
* Cover idempotent and transactional recovery, offset/epoch truncation, concurrent Fetch/upload/reclaim and pinned readers, queue exhaustion, shutdown and disk-full backpressure before broadening the supported fast path.
